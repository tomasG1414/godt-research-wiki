# Single-Shot Quantitative Phase Gradient Microscopy Using a System of Multifunctional Metasurfaces

**Citation:** H. Kwon, E. Arbabi, S. M. Kamali, M. Faraji-Dana, A. Faraon, "Single-shot quantitative phase gradient microscopy using a system of multifunctional metasurfaces," Nature Photonics 14, 109–114 (2020) (published online 2019). Caltech. Cited as ref. 54 in [[2411-08423-godt]].

**Gist:** A DIC-inspired quantitative phase gradient microscope (QPGM) built entirely from two cascaded dielectric metasurface layers, with a total optical-system volume on the order of **1 mm³**. Captures three phase-offset DIC images simultaneously (via polarization + spatial multiplexing) so a quantitative phase gradient image is recovered in a **single shot** with no moving parts, SLM, or liquid-crystal retarder. The [[gradient-phase-imaging]] family member optimized for miniaturization rather than for thick-sample imaging or 3D reconstruction.

## Key results

- **Optical architecture**: metasurface layer 1 is a polarization-sensitive bifocal lens (TE and TM focal points separated along $y$ — this separation *is* the shear) that also spatially splits light three ways; metasurface layer 2 is three birefringent off-axis lenses, each imposing a different TE/TM phase offset. Each layer-2 lens therefore constitutes a complete DIC microscope in combination with layer 1, giving three DIC images at once with 45°/−45° input/output polarizers.
- **Gradient recovery** by three-step phase shifting:
$$\nabla_y\phi = \frac{1}{\Delta y}\arctan\left(\sqrt{3}\,\frac{I_2-I_3}{2I_1-I_2-I_3}\right) - \nabla_y\phi_{\text{cali}}$$
where $I_j = |U(x,y) - e^{i\phi_j}U(x,y-\Delta y)|$ with $\phi_j = \phi_0 + \frac{2\pi}{3}(j-1)$, and $\Delta y$ is the shear. Same sheared-copy interference structure as [[nguyen2017|GLIM]] and [[2411-08423-godt|GODT]], but with the three phase offsets multiplexed *spatially* rather than acquired *sequentially* — this is what buys single-shot operation.
- Phase gradient sensitivity better than **92.3 mrad μm⁻¹**, with single-cell resolution; validated against AFM measurements on phase resolution targets and demonstrated on biological samples (sea urchin).
- **Single shear direction only** ($y$): the paper measures "the unidirectional gradient of the phase sample with respect to $y$" — the same single-shear orientation-dependence that appears throughout this family and that [[2411-08423-godt]] flags as its known limitation.
- **2D only, and the proposed 3D route is z-scanning**: the outlook section suggests reaching tomographic QPI by integrating "different focus scanning schemes... with fast axial scanning," or alternatively capturing three axially-displaced images to apply the transport-of-intensity equation. Neither is a rigorous diffraction-tomographic inversion — this is exactly the z-scanning-for-depth pattern [[2411-08423-godt]] characterizes as the gap it fills.
- Acknowledged limitation: small field of view, arising from the near-4f configuration requiring lens separation comparable to the sum of focal lengths; mitigation suggested via folded-metasurface platforms.

## Method

Dielectric (amorphous silicon nanopost) metasurfaces enabling independent control of transmission phase for two orthogonal polarizations; polarization multiplexing plus spatial multiplexing in a single layer; two implementations tested — metasurfaces on two separate substrates (larger aperture/FOV) and on both sides of one substrate (more compact). Purely optical phase-offset generation; no active modulation elements.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: cited by [[2411-08423-godt]] (ref 54) as the "metasurface enabled microscopy" member of the [[gradient-phase-imaging]] family. Its contribution is complementary to GODT's — miniaturization and single-shot acquisition rather than reconstruction theory — and it fits the GODT paper's framing of the family: a single-direction 2D phase gradient, with z-scanning (or TIE) proposed as the route to depth.

The genuinely transferable idea for the multi-shear extension is **spatial multiplexing of phase offsets**: Kwon et al. get three phase-shifted DIC images in one exposure by splitting the beam three ways through separate metasurface lenses. If a multi-shear GODT scheme needs several shear directions per illumination angle, an analogous multiplexing approach (splitting into parallel shear channels rather than acquiring them sequentially) would keep acquisition time from multiplying — compare the alternative single-shot route via Hilbert demodulation noted in [[2024-06-14-598984-polarization-hilbert-holotomography]]. Whether polarization-grating hardware admits such multiplexing is unexamined *(inference)*.

## Raises

- The three-step phase-shifting formula here recovers the gradient from three intensity measurements; GODT uses five-frame TPS. Whether GODT's demodulation could be reduced to three frames (or one, via multiplexing/Hilbert methods) is a practical acquisition-speed question relevant to multi-shear feasibility, not addressed by any ingested source.
