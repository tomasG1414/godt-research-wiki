# Gradient Optical Diffraction Tomography (preprint)

> **Superseded.** This is the arXiv preprint (2411.08423v1). The peer-reviewed version is [[s42005-025-02416-3]] (*Communications Physics* **8**, 510, 2025), which should be cited instead. The published version adds a finite-shear generalization of the theory, a shear-value study, expanded related work, and reports a different experimental shear (0.8 μm vs. 0.33 μm here). This page is retained for the preprint-only content and to document what changed.

**Citation:** J. Winnik, P. Zdańkowski, M. Stefaniuk, A. Ahmad, C. Zuo, B. S. Ahluwalia, M. Trusiak, "Gradient Optical Diffraction Tomography," arXiv:2411.08423 (2024); later published as *Communications Physics* 8, 510 (2025). Warsaw University of Technology / Nencki Institute / UiT Norway / Nanjing UST.

**Gist:** Introduces GODT, a diffraction-tomographic method that reconstructs the *derivative* of the 3D refractive-index (RI) field directly from shearing data, rather than the field itself. It feeds common-path shearing-interferometry phase-gradient data directly into a first-order [[rytov-approximation|Rytov]] tomographic reconstruction, instead of first integrating the gradient into a phase map (as prior gradient QPI methods do). Because low-coherence LED illumination is compatible with the small shear this requires, GODT gets both the diffraction-compensated, sharp 3D reconstruction of classical [[optical-diffraction-tomography|ODT]] and the high contrast/low-noise character of [[gradient-phase-imaging|gradient-based phase imaging]], applicable even to dense, confluent samples where total-shear common-path ODT fails.

## Key results

- **Central theoretical result** (Eq. 19–21): if $\tilde{O}$ is the 3D scattering-potential spectrum recovered by ODT (Wolf/Ewald-sphere theory, see [[optical-diffraction-tomography]]), and $\tilde{O}_\Delta$ is what you get by running the *same* reconstruction pipeline on shearing-interferometry data (phase-gradient-derived Rytov fields) instead of full-field Rytov data, then $\tilde{O}_\Delta(K_x,K_y,K_z) = iK_x\,\tilde{O}(K_x,K_y,K_z)$ — i.e., GODT output is the exact derivative of the ODT scattering potential in the shear direction, with the tomographic (diffraction-compensating) machinery fully intact. Combined with the near-linear $n \approx n_0 - \tfrac{n_0}{2}O$ relation, GODT delivers a quantitative derivative-of-RI reconstruction.
- Numerical Shepp-Logan phantom validation (180 illumination angles, 60° cone, λ=0.5 μm, NA=1.33): GODT reconstruction matches the finite-difference derivative of the ground truth and matches the numerically-differentiated ODT reconstruction — closing the loop on the theory.
- Amplitude information matters: with the shear-derivative amplitude term included, GODT reconstruction is clean; assuming uniform amplitude (phase-only, the practical case) introduces diffraction-artifact fringes in out-of-focus planes but preserves structural content.
- Experimental validation on a calibrated nano-printed cell phantom ([[nano-printed-cell-phantom]]) in both total-shear (ODT) and gradient (GODT) configurations from the same polarization-grating common-path system: GODT gives visibly higher contrast for subtle RI features and tolerates a lower-coherence LED source (530 nm, Δλ=35 nm) vs. the supercontinuum laser (633 nm, Δλ=6 nm) needed for total-shear mode.
- Applied to fixed hippocampal neuronal cell cultures — too confluent/dense for total-shear common-path imaging (replicas overlap) — GODT still works because it only needs small shear, resolving intracellular structure at multiple depths. This is presented as the key practical payoff: GODT extends common-path self-referencing ODT to dense/confluent samples that total-shear cannot handle.

## Method

- Hardware: linearly-polarized oblique illumination → NA 1.45 100× objective → tube lens → shearing module (SM) of two polarization diffraction gratings (PG1, PG2, period Γ=6.29 μm) that split the beam into two opposite-circular-polarization copies and recombine them parallel, with shear set by grating separation ($\Delta_M = 2z\tan\theta$; effective shear 0.33 μm after 100× magnification, ≈5 camera pixels).
- Phase-gradient demodulation via 5-frame temporal phase shifting: a linear polarizer before the camera is rotated (0°,45°,90°,135°,180°); because the two sheared beams have opposite circular polarization, polarizer rotation by α introduces a 2α interferometric phase shift.
- Reconstruction: standard first-order Rytov + direct-inversion (DI) Ewald-sphere-cap filling ([[optical-diffraction-tomography]]), applied unmodified to the shearing-derived Rytov field instead of the standard Rytov field.
- Same hardware can be reconfigured for total-shear (large Δ, needs high-temporal-coherence source) or gradient/small-shear (low-coherence LED, GODT) mode by adjusting grating separation.
- Simulation forward model uses the wave propagation method of [[brenner1993]] to generate the 180-angle synthetic dataset.

## Related work added in the published version

[[vishnyakov2016]] (Optics Letters, 2016) reconstructed a 3D index derivative from lateral-shearing, low-coherence gradient data under angular illumination scanning. The published version [[s42005-025-02416-3]] cites it as ref. 58 with an explicit comparison — the prior method "does not incorporate diffraction during tomographic reconstruction, which limits its capability for volumetric imaging". The distinguishing factor is diffraction incorporation: DICT uses Radon/straight-ray tomography (justified by its sample's low Δn≈0.02), while GODT works in the Rytov/Ewald diffraction framework.

## Relevance

Per project [[godt]]: this is the core paper (2411.08423) the whole project is organized around. It fixes the theoretical target for the reconstruction pipeline (Eq. 19 is the load-bearing result) and defines the experimental system (polarization-grating common-path shearing module) that a multi-shear extension would need to either replicate or generalize. The paper explicitly flags **single shear direction → orientation-dependent contrast** as a known limitation and names **combining multiple shear orientations** as future work (Sec. 2.2), which is the multi-shear extension this project explores. Also worth noting for that extension: the amplitude-derivative term (Eq. 14) is dropped in practice (phase-only assumption), which is the source of the residual diffraction-artifact fringes — a multi-shear method might revisit whether combined shears recover enough amplitude information to avoid that.

## Raises

- How exactly would multi-shear data be combined in $\tilde{O}$-space — averaged after independent reconstruction, or fused before the inverse FFT? Not addressed by this paper; open question for the simulation thread.
- The paper cites a related QWLI-based ODT concept (ref 61, Yuan et al.) that integrates phase gradients before standard ODT; GODT's approach skips that integration.
- Ref 83 (Zdańkowski et al., bioRxiv 2024.06.14.598984) uses the same polarization-grating common-path hardware — see [[2024-06-14-598984-polarization-hilbert-holotomography]].
- Open question: how to validate the absolute magnitude of a reconstructed index derivative, beyond geometric and structural agreement.
