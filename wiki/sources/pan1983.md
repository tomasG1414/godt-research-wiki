# A Computational Study of Reconstruction Algorithms for Diffraction Tomography: Interpolation Versus Filtered Backpropagation

**Citation:** S. X. Pan and A. C. Kak, "A computational study of reconstruction algorithms for diffraction tomography: Interpolation versus filtered backpropagation," IEEE Trans. Acoust., Speech, Signal Process. ASSP-31(5), 1262–1275 (1983).

**Gist:** Head-to-head computational comparison of direct-Fourier-interpolation reconstruction (what [[2411-08423-godt]] and this wiki call [[optical-diffraction-tomography|direct inversion, DI]]) against Devaney's filtered-backpropagation (FBP) algorithm, on the Shepp-Logan-derived circular test phantom. Conclusion: bilinear-interpolation direct Fourier reconstruction matches FBP's image quality at roughly **1/14th the computational cost** — this is the origin of DI being the default practical choice in ODT, including in GODT.

## Key results

- Confirms the **Fourier diffraction projection theorem** (their name for [[wolf1969]]'s result, restated for both Born and Rytov): the Fourier transform of forward-scattered field data measured on a line perpendicular to plane-wave illumination gives the object's 2D Fourier transform along a semicircular arc of radius $k_0$ (the analog of the Fourier slice theorem in X-ray CT, but curved rather than straight due to diffraction). Multiple illumination angles fill a disk of radius $\sqrt{2}k_0$ in frequency space — object recoverable only up to that spatial-frequency bound (a **low-pass** limit, generally negligible compared to aperture-imposed resolution limits in practice).
- Both Born and Rytov give this same $\sqrt{2}k_0$-disk coverage; the two differ in what's measured (complex scattered-field amplitude for Born vs. unwrapped phase for Rytov) and in numerical superiority (Rytov noted here as numerically superior under noiseless conditions, citing Kaveh, Soumekh & Mueller 1981 — an earlier, less rigorous predecessor to [[chen1998]]'s later quantitative validity study).
- Compares four reconstruction strategies at fixed 64-projection, 128-samples/projection data:
  - **Nearest-neighbor (NN) interpolation** — direct Fourier reconstruction, cheapest, worst quality (ring/interference artifacts).
  - **Bilinear interpolation** — direct Fourier reconstruction, simple, and found to be **as accurate as FBP** once the (K,φ)-plane sampling density is pre-densified 8× via zero-padded inverse-FFT before interpolating.
  - **Circular-sampling-theorem interpolation** (exact in principle, truncated in practice) — more expensive (more neighbors used per point) but *does not* remove the interpolation artifacts NN/bilinear already avoid — diminishing/no returns for the added cost.
  - **Filtered backpropagation (FBP)**, Devaney 1982 — conceptually elegant (direct analog of X-ray filtered backprojection) but requires ~$N^2$ FFTs for an N×N reconstruction with N projections (vs. ~4N for bilinear-interpolation DI) because depth-dependent filtering is needed at every reconstruction depth for every projection.
- Measured on a VAX-11/780: bilinear-interpolation DI reconstruction took **2.2 min** CPU time vs. **30 min** for full FBP — comparable image quality, ~14× cost difference. A "modified FBP" variant (single depth-optimized filter instead of depth-dependent filtering) cuts FBP's cost to 2.8 min but only gives locally-accurate reconstruction near the chosen depth, not globally.
- **Overall recommendation**: bilinear interpolation followed by direct Fourier inversion (i.e., [[optical-diffraction-tomography|DI]]) should be preferred over FBP for diffraction-tomography reconstruction whenever the Born/Rytov weak-scattering approximation itself is valid — the reconstruction-algorithm choice is a computational-cost decision, not an accuracy one, once approximation validity is assumed.

## Method

Simulated 2D circular test phantom (superposition of ellipses, same gray-level convention as Devaney's earlier circular test image, visually identical to the classic Shepp-Logan head phantom) with analytically known Fourier transform (closed-form Bessel-function expression per ellipse) — lets diffracted-projection data be generated exactly rather than via full wave simulation. 64 projections × 128 samples/projection standard test configuration across all four reconstruction methods compared.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: this is the primary source for why [[optical-diffraction-tomography|DI/direct inversion]] (not FBP) is the default ODT reconstruction algorithm that [[2411-08423-godt]] builds on (its ref 72). Its bilinear-interpolation-with-pre-densification recipe is a good default for any DI implementation, including this project's simulations, where nearest-neighbour gridding turned out to be a large error term ([[multiple-scattering-check]]).
