# Comparative Study of Iterative Reconstruction Algorithms for Missing Cone Problems in Optical Diffraction Tomography

**Citation:** J. Lim, K. Lee, K. H. Jin, S. Shin, S. Lee, Y. Park, J. C. Ye, "Comparative study of iterative reconstruction algorithms for missing cone problems in optical diffraction tomography," Optics Express 23(13), 16933–16948 (2015). KAIST.

**Gist:** Systematic head-to-head comparison of three regularized iterative reconstruction algorithms for mitigating the [[missing-cone-problem]] in ODT — Gerchberg-Papoulis (GP, non-negativity only), edge-preserving (EP) regularization, and total-variation (TV) regularization — on both numerical microsphere phantoms and real biological samples (RBCs, hepatocytes), across low and high effective NA. No single algorithm wins universally: TV is best for homogeneous samples, EP better for textured/inhomogeneous ones, GP is the weakest but simplest baseline.

## Key results

- Confirms missing-cone problem's universality: appears identically whether the ODT reconstruction algorithm is projection-based (straight-ray) or diffraction-based (Ewald-sphere/[[wolf1969]]-theorem), and is a known issue shared with X-ray CT, EM, and MRI reconstruction from incomplete angular data — ODT is not special in the underlying math, just under-explored in the ODT-specific literature per this paper's framing (as of 2015).
- **GP (Gerchberg-Papoulis)**: cheapest (fewest iterations, ~15-22s), enforces only non-negativity, but leaves substantial axial elongation (widest RI axial profile: 8.52 μm on a 5μm phantom at NAeff=0.5, only narrowing to 6.20-6.29 μm at NAeff=0.8) and is noise-sensitive — worst performer on both metrics across nearly every test.
- **EP (edge-preserving, Charbonnier/Delaney-Bresler half-quadratic regularization)**: intermediate cost (~170-220s), better denoising while preserving real edges, best overall for biological samples with genuine internal texture (RBC cytoplasm, hepatocyte cytoplasm) — but its performance is more NA-dependent than TV's.
- **TV (total variation, split-Bregman)**: most expensive (~365-510s), narrowest RI distributions and best axial-elongation reduction on homogeneous/piecewise-constant samples (microsphere: axial width 6.29→4.97 μm as NAeff goes 0.5→0.8, best of the three), least NA-dependent — but introduces visible **cartoon-like/stair-step artifacts** on samples with genuine smooth internal gradients (hepatocyte cytoplasm), i.e. it's the wrong tool exactly for the kind of sample [[krauze2016]]'s GTVIC was designed to handle (non-piecewise-constant biological structure).
- All three algorithms increase reconstructed RI toward the true value relative to plain inverse-FFT (FT) reconstruction, but by different amounts and with different failure modes — there is no free lunch; algorithm choice should depend on whether the sample is closer to piecewise-constant (favor TV) or has genuine smooth gradients/texture (favor EP), a direct, quantified version of the same tradeoff [[krauze2016]]'s GTVIC tries to sidestep via its adaptive shape-mask approach rather than committing to one regularizer's bias.
- Effective NA matters a great deal independent of algorithm: axial elongation improves substantially from NAeff=0.5 to 0.8 for all three regularizers, confirming (quantitatively) that a wider illumination cone directly shrinks the missing region, consistent with [[wolf1969]]'s original geometric argument.
- Noise robustness (SNR 5dB/10dB tests): GP remains most noise-sensitive; EP and TV both denoise effectively while regularizing.

## Method

Rytov-approximation ODT theory restated per [[wolf1969]]'s Fourier diffraction theorem (their Eq. 5, directly citing Wolf). Reconstruction cost functions: GP = non-negativity-only iterative refinement; EP = half-quadratic regularized least-squares with an edge-preserving potential function, solved by alternating minimization + conjugate gradient; TV = $\ell_1$-norm-of-gradient regularization with non-negativity, solved via split-Bregman iteration. Tested on: simulated 5μm homogeneous fused-silica-index microsphere phantom; real fused-silica microspheres; real RBCs and hepatocytes (Huh-7 line) — Mach-Zehnder tomographic phase microscopy, 532nm, >300 illumination angles in spiral scan pattern, two effective-NA conditions (0.5 and 0.8) via galvo-mirror voltage limiting.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: an independent (KAIST) data point on missing-cone mitigation, complementing [[krauze2016]]'s GTVIC: no regularizer wins universally, and the choice depends on how piecewise-constant the sample is.
