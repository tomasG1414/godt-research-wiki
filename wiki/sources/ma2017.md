# Optical Tomographic Reconstruction Based on Multi-Slice Wave Propagation Method

**Citation:** X. Ma, W. Xiao, F. Pan, "Optical tomographic reconstruction based on multi-slice wave propagation method," Optics Express 25(19), 22595–22607 (2017). Beihang University.

**Gist:** Uses the wave propagation method ([[brenner1993]]) as a **nonlinear forward model inside a tomographic reconstruction loop**, solved by FISTA optimization, to handle multiply-scattering objects that break the weak-scattering (Born/Rytov) linearization underlying conventional [[optical-diffraction-tomography|ODT]]. Positions WPM as strictly more accurate than the beam propagation method (BPM) at large angles and strong RI contrast — and notes WPM "has not been introduced in tomographic reconstruction before" this work.

## Key results

- **The accuracy ladder for forward models**, as laid out here: ray approximation (no scattering, very weak contrast only) → first-order Born/Rytov (single scattering, linear, this is what [[s42005-025-02416-3|GODT]] uses) → nonlinear multi-slice models (multiple scattering). Diffraction tomography's accuracy "decreases" once objects are thick or contain fast-varying structure, because multiple scattering violates the linearization.
- **Why WPM over BPM.** Both are multi-slice. BPM splits the propagation operator into homogeneous-medium propagation plus a thin-element transmission through Δn, which invokes the slowly-varying-envelope approximation and confines BPM to paraxial propagation and small RI variation. WPM does not split the operator — it decomposes the field into plane-wave components and propagates each non-paraxially through the inhomogeneous medium, then integrates. Hence higher accuracy at large angles, strong contrast, and fast-varying structure. This is the same BPM-vs-WPM comparison [[brenner1993]] makes, now carried into reconstruction rather than pure simulation.
- **Reconstruction method**: iterative optimization with FISTA (fast iterative shrinkage-thresholding), with the paper's main technical contribution being an efficient scheme for computing the transmitted field *and its derivative* through the WPM forward model — the derivative being what makes gradient-based optimization tractable given WPM's cost.
- Experimentally demonstrated to address multiple-scattering problems with high accuracy on complex fast-varying structures, with discussion of how to choose the method's parameters.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: connects two threads already in the wiki. [[brenner1993]] supplies WPM as GODT's *forward simulation* engine (used to generate the synthetic Shepp-Logan dataset); this paper shows the same model can serve as the forward operator inside an *inverse* problem, with derivatives computed efficiently enough for iterative optimization.

That matters for the multi-shear extension in a specific way. If the extension goes the optimization route — as in [[2509-15382-osi-flex]], and the natural way to fuse multiple shear channels — then the forward model becomes a design choice rather than a fixed assumption. Two options are then available:

- Keep the **linear Rytov/Ewald** model (GODT's own), where each shear applies a known $\frac{e^{j\Delta K}-1}{\Delta}$ factor. Simpler, analytically clean, preserves the "rigorous framework" framing that distinguishes GODT from [[vishnyakov2016]].
- Adopt a **nonlinear WPM** forward model as here, gaining validity for thicker/denser samples at substantial computational cost.

The second is more relevant than it might appear, since GODT's stated advantage is precisely imaging *dense, thick, confluent* samples — exactly the regime where [[chen1998]]'s weak-scattering bounds start to bite and where this paper argues linear models degrade. Whether GODT's actual samples cross that threshold is unquantified by any ingested source *(inference)*.

Note the compute warning from [[brenner1993]]: WPM is ~100× slower than BPM for forward simulation alone. Inside an iterative reconstruction loop over ~180 illumination angles and multiple shear channels, that cost compounds sharply.

## Raises

- No ingested source establishes where GODT's samples (neurons, [[nano-printed-cell-phantom]]) fall relative to the single-scattering validity boundary. This is the same gap flagged earlier in this wiki regarding diffraction compensation, approached from the opposite direction — there, whether GODT needs *more* than straight-ray; here, whether it needs more than *single-scattering*.
- Combining a nonlinear forward model with GODT's shear-derivative measurement is unexamined: the clean $\tilde{O}_\Delta = iK_x\tilde{O}$ relation depends on the linearity of the Rytov mapping, and it is not obvious what replaces it in a nonlinear multi-slice setting.
