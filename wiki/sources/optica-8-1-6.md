# Quantitative 3D Refractive Index Tomography of Opaque Samples in Epi-Mode

**Citation:** P. Ledwig, F. E. Robles, "Quantitative 3D refractive index tomography of opaque samples in epi-mode," Optica 8(1), 6–14 (2021). Georgia Tech / Emory. Cited as ref. 56 in [[2411-08423-godt]].

**Gist:** Extends quantitative oblique back-illumination microscopy (qOBM) — a differential-phase-contrast technique using epi-illumination and multiply-scattered light as a virtual transmissive source — from 2D phase imaging into full 3D RI tomography, via a rigorously derived non-paraxial 3D optical transfer function (OTF) and deconvolution. Unlike transmission-based [[optical-diffraction-tomography|ODT]] (including [[2411-08423-godt|GODT]]), this works on **opaque, arbitrarily-thick scattering samples** (e.g., bulk brain tissue) — the tradeoff GODT's shearing/common-path family cannot make, since GODT still needs transmissive access to see the object beam.

## Key results

- **qOBM principle**: 4 LEDs at canted angles feed multimode fibers around a standard inverted bright-field microscope objective; illuminating with diametrically-opposed LED pairs and subtracting the resulting images gives a differential-phase-contrast signal analogous to DIC, but generated by *multiply-scattered* light acting as an effective transmissive virtual source inside an otherwise opaque sample — no coherent laser, no interferometer.
- **3D extension's core theory**: derives a non-paraxial 3D OTF $T^{3D}_V(q,q_z)$ (Eq. 13) for the scattering potential $V(\vec{r})=k^2(1-n'(\vec{r})^2)$, built from the same weak-phase-object/first-Born-type approximation as standard ODT, but generalized to an arbitrary (measured, Monte-Carlo-simulated) angular source distribution $S(u)$ rather than a single plane wave — this produces **two Ewald shells** (conjugate pair) per source component, directly generalizing the single-Ewald-cap picture in [[wolf1969]]/[[optical-diffraction-tomography]] to partially-coherent, multiply-scattered illumination.
- Reconstruction via Tikhonov-regularized direct deconvolution (efficient, used for full wide-field volumes) or total-variation-constrained iterative deconvolution (higher fidelity, but **~372-500× slower** — the same TV cost/quality tradeoff documented for standard ODT in [[lim2015]] and [[krauze2016]]).
- Same [[missing-cone-problem]] signature appears here too: direct Tikhonov deconvolution retains axial spread/elongation from the "missing cone" high-axial-frequency gap; TV-constrained iterative reconstruction largely removes it (near-perfect bead reconstruction) at large compute cost — independent confirmation that this artifact is generic to limited-angular-diversity 3D reconstruction, not specific to any one illumination geometry.
- Validated on 10μm polystyrene beads under scattering intralipid-agar phantoms and fresh thick brain tissue (nearly identical results between the two, confirming robustness to real scattering media), then demonstrated on unaltered fresh human cortex and rat brain: full 243×243×104 μm volumes resolving axonal cross-sections, extracellular vesicles, and neuronal nuclei at up to ~100 μm depth (≈1 mean free scattering path length) — a field of view roughly an order of magnitude larger than typical transmission ODT (~50×50 μm) and with no upper bound on sample thickness (opaque/semi-infinite samples workable, unlike thin transmissive ODT samples).
- 2D-vs-3D reconstruction comparison directly demonstrates why a 2D "phase stack" model of a thick sample is inadequate: it misassigns/loses low-frequency content because it ignores the curved (Ewald-sphere-like) pupil-function geometry that only a genuine 3D model captures — producing a hollow-looking bead reconstruction where the true 3D model gives a solid one.

## Method

Non-interferometric: intensity-only through-focus image stacks (Δz≈0.35-0.5μm) under 4 canted-LED (720nm) epi-illumination, far-red incoherent sources, standard 60× NA 0.7 long-working-distance objective. Source angular distribution $S(u)$ obtained via Monte Carlo photon-transport simulation of bulk tissue scattering (not measured directly) — an important methodological difference from GODT's directly-measured coherent illumination angles.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: cited directly by [[2411-08423-godt]] (ref 56) as part of the broader "oblique back-illuminated microscopy" branch of the [[gradient-phase-imaging]] family (differential/DPC-like contrast from paired oblique illumination, structurally similar in spirit to GODT's shearing-derived gradient signal, though qOBM's "gradient" comes from illumination-pair subtraction rather than beam-shearing). The two share the missing-cone/axial-elongation problem and the TV-regularization cost/quality tradeoff as generic consequences of limited-angle 3D reconstruction, independent of illumination modality. Not directly applicable hardware for GODT (incoherent, non-interferometric, opaque-sample regime) but a useful reference for how a fundamentally different measurement geometry hits the same reconstruction-theory walls GODT does — reinforces that these limitations (missing cone, TV cost) are properties of the *inverse problem*, not of any specific optical implementation.

## Raises

- qOBM's illumination-pair-subtraction gradient and GODT's beam-shearing gradient are both "differential" measurements feeding into an Ewald-sphere-type reconstruction, but the underlying transfer-function derivations differ (arbitrary partially-coherent source distribution here vs. GODT's coherent plane-wave shearing). Whether qOBM's non-paraxial 3D OTF framework offers a useful generalization for a multi-shear GODT extension (e.g., treating multiple shear directions as an effective "source distribution" the way qOBM treats its LED geometry) is speculative *(inference)* — no ingested source draws this connection directly.
