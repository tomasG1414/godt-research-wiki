# Deep Learning-Based Single-Shot Lateral Shearing Interferometry

**Citation:** M. T. Nguyen, H.-M. Park, K.-N. Joo, Y.-S. Ghim, "Deep learning-based single-shot lateral shearing interferometry," Optics and Lasers in Engineering 191, 109010 (2025). KRISS / Chosun University, South Korea.

**Gist:** Builds an x- and y-directional lateral shearing interferometer from **two polarization-grating shearing modules in a single setup**, captures both shear directions superimposed as one composite interferogram, and uses a deep neural network (DYnet++) to demultiplex the two directions' phases from that single frame. Demonstrates single-shot and dynamic surface metrology. Directly relevant to the multi-shear extension: it establishes that polarization-grating optics — the same component family [[s42005-025-02416-3|GODT]] uses — can produce two shear directions in one instrument.

## Key results

- **Hardware: two-direction shear from polarization gratings.** Extends Jeong et al.'s basic PG shearing module by adding a second flat-mirror + polarization-grating pair, "which enables the formation of the x- and y- shearing parts in a single setup." Each PG is mounted on a flat mirror; a circularly-polarized beam passing through the PG twice (via mirror reflection) is laterally shifted, in a reflective double-pass arrangement. Optical shutters (OSx, OSy) allow either direction to be isolated for training-data collection.
- **The multiplexing problem, and a learned solution.** With both shutters open, the x- and y- shearing interferograms overlap into one composite pattern on a pixelated polarizer camera. Rather than separating them optically (spatial multiplexing, as in [[sciadv-adl0501]]) or spectrally (carrier-frequency separation, as in QWLSI), a modified DYnet++ network takes the composite frame and outputs the arctangent numerators and denominators for each direction separately, from which both wrapped phase maps follow. Training data generated by driving a deformable mirror to produce many known wavefronts.
- **Why the alternatives were rejected** — useful comparative context: sequential x/y acquisition (including Jeong et al.'s PG method, which needs the shearing device rotated 90°) precludes single-shot; Luo et al.'s dual-LSI requires separating fundamental and zero frequencies in the Fourier domain, risking errors from imperfect separation; **QWLSI** generates four sheared beams from a 2D grating with distinct carrier frequencies enabling Fourier-domain separation, but "requires spatially coherent illumination to achieve high-contrast interferograms due to its high spatial carrier frequency."
- Validated experimentally on surfaces from simple concave to complex random, with surface reconstruction from the x/y gradient maps via standard modal/zonal algorithms.

## Method

Optical: BS1/BS2, sample, linear polarizer, two shutter-gated PG+flat-mirror shearing arms (x and y), imaging lens, pixelated polarizer camera. Computational: modified DYnet++ deep network for composite-interferogram demultiplexing; conventional zonal/modal integration for surface reconstruction from gradients.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: this is the best available evidence on the **hardware feasibility** of multi-direction shear with polarization gratings, which was an open question raised while ingesting [[sciadv-adl0501]]. The answer is that adding a second PG shearing arm is sufficient — no exotic component required — though this paper's double-pass reflective geometry differs from GODT's transmissive PG1/PG2 pair and would need adapting.

It also lays out the design space for handling multiple shear channels, which a multi-shear GODT must choose among:

- **Sequential acquisition** — simplest, but multiplies GODT's already-large acquisition burden (5 frames × 180 angles) by the number of shear directions.
- **Spatial multiplexing** — separate image regions per shear direction ([[sciadv-adl0501]]); costs field of view or sensor area.
- **Carrier-frequency multiplexing** — QWLSI-style; **incompatible with GODT's low-coherence LED illumination**, since it needs spatially coherent light for high-contrast high-carrier fringes. This is a genuine constraint worth recording: the frequency-domain route is closed for GODT specifically because of the coherence choice that makes GODT attractive in the first place.
- **Computational demultiplexing** — this paper's approach; preserves full field of view and single-shot operation at the cost of requiring training data and accepting a learned (non-deterministic, harder-to-characterize) inversion step.

Note the application domain differs substantially — specular surface metrology with a deformable-mirror training source, not weakly-scattering biological samples — so the network itself would not transfer, and generating equivalent training data for tomographic biological imaging is non-trivial.

## Raises

- Would a learned demultiplexer be acceptable inside a GODT pipeline whose selling point is a rigorous analytical framework? Mixing a deterministic Rytov/Ewald inversion with a learned preprocessing step weakens the "fully rigorous" framing that distinguishes GODT from prior art (see [[vishnyakov2016]] discussion). A design tension worth being explicit about if the extension goes this route.
- This paper's PG modules operate in double-pass reflection; GODT's operate in transmission. Whether two transmissive PG pairs at different orientations can be cascaded without the first pair's shear corrupting the second's is unexamined by any ingested source.
