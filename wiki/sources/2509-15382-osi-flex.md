# OSI-flex: Optimization-Based Shearing Interferometry for Joint Phase and Shear Estimation Using a Flexible Open-Source Framework

**Citation:** J. Winnik, D. Suski, M. Heto, M. Lenarnik, M. Ziemczonok, M. Trusiak, P. Zdańkowski, "OSI-flex: optimization-based shearing interferometry for joint phase and shear estimation using a flexible open-source framework," ***Journal of Physics: Photonics*, DOI 10.1088/2515-7647/ae3504 (published 2026-01-07)**. Warsaw University of Technology. Code: **github.com/QCI-LAB/OSI-flex**. Ingested from the arXiv preprint (arXiv:2509.15382, 2025-09-18).

> **Cite the published version.** The ingested copy was the preprint; a peer-reviewed version exists (found via the [[forward-citation-sweep-2026-07|July 2026 citation sweep]]). The preprint has not been re-checked against it, so differences between the two are unknown — worth a diff before relying on specific numbers.

**Gist:** An open-source, optimization-based framework from **the GODT authors themselves** for recovering absolute phase from sheared phase-difference measurements. Uses automatic differentiation (TensorFlow) plus the ADAM optimizer to **jointly refine the phase and estimate the shear vectors**, and — critically for this project — supports **arbitrary numbers, magnitudes, and orientations of shear vectors**. This is the closest thing in the corpus to existing tooling for the multi-shear problem, albeit for 2D phase integration rather than 3D tomographic reconstruction.

## Key results

- **Formulation.** Given $N$ measured phase differences $\Delta\varphi_n^M$ with shear vectors $\mathbf{d}_n$, minimize
$$\mathcal{E}(\varphi;\mathbf{d},\Delta\varphi^M) = \mathcal{D}(\varphi;\mathbf{d},\Delta\varphi^M) + \mathcal{R}(\varphi)$$
with an L2/MSE data-fidelity term summed over all $N$ shear channels, and regularization $\mathcal{R} = \alpha_{TV}TV(\varphi) + \alpha_{SP}SP(\varphi)$ combining total variation with a sign penalty enforcing non-negativity/non-positivity.
- **Sub-pixel shear handling.** Non-integer shears are evaluated by bilinear interpolation among the four nearest integer-shear phase differences (Eq. 4), deliberately constructed to be piecewise-differentiable *with respect to the shear parameters* — which is what makes shear itself optimizable by gradient descent alongside the phase.
- **Joint shear estimation matters quantitatively.** Simulations show a shear error as small as **0.1 pixel** produces noticeable phase errors, while the standard alternative (cross-correlation shear detection) is only accurate to ~0.5 pixels and becomes unreliable at small shear magnitudes because correlation peaks sit too close together. Since shear is hard to determine precisely in most real systems, joint optimization is presented as necessary rather than merely convenient.
- **Flexibility across shear configurations.** Optimal reconstruction still comes from two orthogonal shears, but TV + sign-constraint regularization keeps OSI-flex effective with non-orthogonal shears and even a **single** shear measurement (enabling dynamic monitoring). Shear magnitudes from sub-pixel (differential/gradient regime) up to several dozen pixels (semi-total-shear regime) are all supported — explicitly contrasted with existing software "restricted to ideally orthogonal, x- and y-oriented, small and known shear magnitudes."
- Validated on simulations, a calibrated phase resolution target, a 3D-printed cell phantom (same phantom lineage as [[ziemczonok2019]]), and follicular thyroid cells. Reports accurate recovery of both low and high spatial frequencies and robustness against the error-propagation and noise-amplification failure modes of direct numerical integration.
- Survey value: the introduction catalogues the integration methods this replaces — direct numerical integration (fast, but needs two small orthogonal shears, accumulates noise), Fourier-transform reconstruction (typically two small orthogonal shears, generalized to orthogonal finite shears by QWLSI work), alternating projections (heuristic, sometimes needing as many as 24 phase-difference measurements), and modal/Zernike least-squares fitting (good for smooth wavefronts, oversmooths real objects).

## Method

TensorFlow implementation; automatic differentiation supplies cost-function gradients with respect to both the phase estimate and the shear vectors, so changing the forward model requires no manual re-derivation. ADAM optimizer with adaptive per-parameter learning rates. Code and test data publicly released.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: the most directly useful source for the project's simulation thread, for three reasons.

1. **Same authors as GODT.** Winnik and Zdańkowski are the GODT co-first authors; Trusiak and Ziemczonok also appear. It handles *multi-shear* data in a flexible, optimization-based, open-source way, so a multi-shear extension can build on it.
2. **The multi-shear machinery already exists in 2D.** OSI-flex handles arbitrary shear counts, magnitudes, and orientations, with joint shear estimation — exactly the flexibility a multi-shear scheme needs. It recovers **2D phase**, while [[s42005-025-02416-3|GODT]] reconstructs in 3D without integrating, so the two address different stages.
3. **The architecture transfers.** An automatic-differentiation + ADAM formulation with a data-fidelity term summed over shear channels is directly analogous to what a multi-shear GODT reconstruction would need, with the forward model mapping a 3D scattering potential through the [[optical-diffraction-tomography|Ewald-sphere]] relation and the per-shear factor $\frac{e^{j\Delta K}-1}{\Delta}$ instead of a 2D phase through pixel shifts. *(inference — the paper makes no tomographic claim.)*

The 0.1-pixel shear-accuracy finding is also a practical warning for the extension: if reconstruction is that sensitive to shear error in 2D, a multi-shear tomographic scheme should probably estimate shear jointly rather than trust nominal values.
