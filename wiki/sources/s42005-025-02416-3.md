# Gradient Optical Diffraction Tomography (published version)

**Citation:** J. Winnik, P. Zdańkowski, M. Stefaniuk, A. Ahmad, C. Zuo, B. S. Ahluwalia, M. Trusiak, "Gradient optical diffraction tomography," *Communications Physics* **8**, 510 (2025). DOI: 10.1038/s42005-025-02416-3.

**Gist:** The peer-reviewed published version of [[2411-08423-godt]] (arXiv:2411.08423). **This is the canonical GODT reference.** Core theory and experiments match the preprint; the published version adds a finite-shear generalization of the theory, a new "Influence of the shear value" study, an expanded related-work discussion (including [[vishnyakov2016]]), and reports the experimental shear as 0.8 μm.

## What changed from the preprint

Four differences matter for this project:

1. **Expanded related work.** The published version discusses gradient-based volumetric methods (refs. 57–61: QLSI-based ODT, directional-DPC + intensity diffraction tomography, numerical-phase-derivative methods), including DIC tomography [[vishnyakov2016]] as ref. 58, which builds a 3D gradient image without modelling diffraction. GODT's contribution is the 3D RI gradient inside the Rytov/Ewald framework ("a first-of-its-kind contrast domain: the 3D RI gradient").

2. **Finite-shear generalization (new, Eqs. 20–21).** The preprint's derivation assumed infinitesimal shear, giving the derivative factor $jK_x$. The published version shows the finite-difference operator has an exact Fourier-domain counterpart:
$$\frac{f(x+\Delta)-f(x)}{\Delta} \;\xleftrightarrow{\;FT\;}\; \frac{e^{j\Delta K_x}-1}{\Delta}\,\tilde f(K_x)$$
and that $jK_x = \lim_{\Delta\to 0}\frac{e^{j\Delta K_x}-1}{\Delta}$ is its special case. So "the tomographic reconstruction process can be formulated using the finite-difference derivative operator without loss of generality" — GODT's theory is valid at arbitrary shear, not only in the small-shear limit.

3. **New "Influence of the shear value" study (Fig. 4).** Compares GODT reconstructions at Δ = 0.1 μm and Δ = 0.8 μm against finite-difference approximations of the ODT derivative at matching shears. Finding: larger shear slightly smooths high-frequency detail, but 3D sectioning capability is preserved and results still match the corresponding finite-difference ODT derivative. Practically, larger shear may be *desirable* (noise reduction) or unavoidable (hardware limits) — GODT tolerates it.

4. **Experimental shear.** Published: grating separation $d_{PG}$ = 0.47 mm, beam shear Δ' = 80 μm, effective shear Δ = 0.8 μm (preprint: 0.2 mm, 33 μm, 0.33 μm). Both satisfy $\Delta' = 2 d_{PG}\tan\theta$. Same LED (530 nm, Δλ = 35 nm), same grating period (Γ = 6.29 μm, θ ≈ 4.83°), same 100× magnification. This wiki uses the published values.

## Other details worth carrying forward

- **Single-shot phase shifting is possible**: "Phase shifted interferograms can be also obtained in a single shot employing a pixelated polarization camera." This is a hardware route to eliminating the multi-frame temporal phase shifting bottleneck — distinct from the Hilbert-transform route in [[2024-06-14-598984-polarization-hilbert-holotomography]] and the spatial-multiplexing route in [[kwon2019]].
- Shear value is tuned by inter-grating distance $d_{PG}$; **fringe density and orientation are controlled by rotating PG2 about the optical axis**.
- Same hardware reconfigures to total-shear mode by increasing $d_{PG}$ — used for the reference ODT measurement of the cell phantom. Explicitly *not* available for the neural-cell sample (no object-free region), which is why no reference ODT comparison is shown there.
- Extended outlook: GODT proposed as a universal framework potentially extensible to X-ray, UV, and IR regimes, and to quantitative 3D stress/strain (gradient-of-displacement) analysis in experimental micro-biomechanics.
- Multi-shear future work, in the Results: "future works may overcome this GODT limitation by combining information obtained for various shear orientations" (the limitation being single-shear directional contrast). The Conclusions go further: "Future research will focus on refining the GODT technique through amplitude map corrections, merging data for two orthogonal shear directions, …"

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: **this should be the project's primary citation**, not the arXiv preprint. Two items bear directly on the multi-shear extension.

First, the finite-shear factor $\frac{e^{j\Delta K_x}-1}{\Delta}$ is a stronger foundation to build on than $jK_x$, because it is exact for the shear actually used experimentally.

Second, that factor **vanishes wherever $\Delta K_x = 2\pi m$ for integer $m \neq 0$**, i.e. at spatial frequencies $K_x = 2\pi m/\Delta$. This null comb is known in 2D ([[servin2007]], [[mehta-sheppard2008]]); [[finite-shear-null-experiment]] quantifies it in 3D and shows that different shear *values* null different frequencies, so combining shears fills each other's nulls.
