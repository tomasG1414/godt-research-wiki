# Three-Dimensional Structure Determination of Semi-Transparent Objects from Holographic Data

**Citation:** E. Wolf, "Three-dimensional structure determination of semi-transparent objects from holographic data," Optics Communications 1(4), 153–156 (1969).

**Gist:** The foundational paper for [[optical-diffraction-tomography]] — a 4-page note deriving what is now called Wolf's generalized projection theorem (a.k.a. the Fourier diffraction theorem): under the first Born approximation for weak scattering, the 2D Fourier transform of a scattered field measured in a plane equals a scaled slice of the 3D Fourier transform of the object's scattering potential, restricted to a spherical surface (later termed the Ewald sphere). This is the theorem [[2411-08423-godt]] and essentially every ODT paper in this project cite as the starting point.

## Key results

- Scattered field $U^{(s)}(R)$ from a weakly-scattering object obeys $(\nabla^2+k_0^2)U^{(s)}(R) = F(R)U(R)$, where $F(R) = -k_0^2[n^2(R)-1]$ is the **scattering potential** (Eq. 4–5) — this is the object function ODT ultimately reconstructs.
- Under the first Born approximation, $U^{(s)}$ is given by an integral of $F$ against a free-space Green's function (Eq. 6), which Wolf rewrites as an angular spectrum of plane waves via a Weyl-type expansion (Eq. 7–10).
- **Central result** (Eq. 15): the 2D Fourier transform of the scattered field measured in a plane $z=z^*$, $\hat{U}^{(s)}_h(u,v;z^*)$, relates to the 3D Fourier transform $\hat{F}$ of the scattering potential by
$$\hat{F}(U,V,W^*) = \frac{k_0}{iw^*}\exp(\mp i w^* z^*)\,\hat{U}^{(s)}_h(u,v;z^*)$$
where $(U,V,W^*) = \mathbf{k}^* - \mathbf{k}_0$ is the difference between the observed and incident wavevectors — i.e., each 2D measurement fills in the 3D object spectrum along a spherical surface set by momentum transfer. Formally identical to the von Laue equations of X-ray structure analysis and the momentum-transfer equation of scattering theory; Bragg's law follows directly.
- Only accessible for **homogeneous** waves ($p^2+q^2<1$, i.e. propagating, not evanescent, components) — this is the origin of the NA/missing-cone limitation in all downstream ODT ([[missing-cone-problem]]).
- Resolution bound: accessible $|K^*| \le 2k_0 = 4\pi/\lambda_0$, giving a theoretical resolution limit $L \ge \lambda_0/2$ (half a wavelength) — but achievable resolution is degraded further by whatever practical method recovers phase/amplitude from the hologram (Wolf notes side-band holography of the era only reached ~9λ due to phase-recovery limitations, not this theorem).
- Note added in proof: rotating the *observation plane* orientation, not just the illumination direction, can in principle reconstruct all 3D Fourier components down to the theoretical limit — an early acknowledgment that angle diversity (of either illumination or detection) is what fills the object spectrum.

## Method

Pure theory note (no experiments): scalar-field derivation (polarization explicitly ignored, "a fuller analysis... will be given in a later publication"), first Born approximation only — does not derive the Rytov variant used by [[2411-08423-godt]] (that comes later via [[chen1998]] and follow-on work). A companion paper (its ref [1]) supplies the practical piece: recovering complex scattered-field amplitude/phase from hologram intensity-transmission measurements.

## Relevance

Per [[godt]]: this is the theorem [[optical-diffraction-tomography]] is built on, and by extension what [[2411-08423-godt]]'s Eq. 2 (the Rytov-approximation restatement of the same relation) descends from. Confirms that the missing-cone/NA limitation GODT inherits ([[missing-cone-problem]]) is present already at the Born-approximation, single-observation-plane level in 1969 — not an artifact introduced by the Rytov reformulation or by GODT's shearing modification.

## Raises

- Wolf's paper is Born-approximation only; GODT explicitly uses Rytov. The bridge between the two (and why Rytov is preferred for ODT) is covered by [[chen1998]], not this paper.
